permits:
  heading: "Permits and rules in Frederick City vs. Frederick County"
  intro: |
    Permit rules in Frederick depend on *where* the tree sits and what else is happening on the lot. This is general guidance for homeowners — not legal advice. We flag likely requirements during the estimate; always verify with the City, County, HOA, or Maryland DNR for your address.
  sections:
    - heading: "Private residential trees (typical backyard case)"
      body: |
        Standalone removal of a tree entirely on private residential property is often not subject to a special municipal "tree removal permit." Exceptions still come up: HOA architectural rules, easements, active grading or development permits, and forest-conservation conditions on some lots.

        Frederick County's Forest Resources Ordinance (Chapter 1-21) is aimed mainly at subdivision, site plan, and larger grading or sediment-control projects — not everyday single-tree yard removals. If your project is tied to a bigger land disturbance, ask the County before you clear.
    - heading: "Roadside and street trees (public right-of-way)"
      body: |
        Trees in the public road right-of-way generally fall under Maryland's Roadside Tree Law. Trimming or removing a roadside tree typically requires a Tree Care Permit through the Maryland DNR Forest Service, and work on taller roadside trees must be done by a licensed tree expert. City street trees are managed under these rules. Do not prune or remove a tree in the public right-of-way on your own. For City of Frederick street-tree questions, contact Urban Forestry / the City Arborist at (301) 600-1233, or start with the City's Urban Forestry resources online.
    - heading: "Historic District (City of Frederick)"
      body: |
        Private-property tree removal inside the City of Frederick Historic District is reviewed by the Historic Preservation Commission (HPC), not Urban Forestry. Check whether your parcel is in the district before you schedule work, and contact Preservation Planning at (301) 600-1499 with questions. We'll note Historic District status when we can spot it at the estimate so you aren't surprised mid-job.
    - heading: "HOAs and forest conservation easements"
      body: |
        Many newer Frederick County subdivisions require HOA or architectural approval even when the City or County does not issue a tree permit. Some lots also carry forest-conservation or easement limits on clearing. Bring any HOA packet or plat notes to the estimate and we'll help you see what still needs homeowner approval before the saws come out.
